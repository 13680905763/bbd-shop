# Customs and Taxes

Generally speaking (except for EU countries), when the declared value of your parcel is lower than the country's tariff threshold, it will generally not be taxed (but fraudulent declaration is another matter). If the parcel is taxed, you need to pay taxes in time and assist in customs clearance.

## Reference for Tariff Thresholds in Various Countries

Tips:

- Switzerland: Tariff threshold 100 Swiss francs, VAT (value-added tax) threshold 65 Swiss francs.
- Customs in Canada and Europe inspect parcels strictly, and commercial couriers such as DHL, UPS, and FedEx are more likely to be subject to tariffs.

## Parcel Submission Tips

- Parcel weight is an important basis for judging whether it is a civilian parcel or a commercial parcel. Parcels of 10 kg and above are more likely to be considered commercial parcels.
- Too many products of the same kind may also be judged as commercial parcels. It is recommended to avoid sending a large number of identical products in one parcel.
- Please consider carefully when sending medicines and comply with customs policies.

*The above information is collected and organized from the Internet and is for reference only and not as standard guidance.*

## Customs Declaration Instructions

- Customs clearance fees for non-duty-free/unsupported IOSS agency service routes are borne by the user.
- Customs basically conducts random checks on parcels. If the parcel is special (large size, heavy weight or contains sensitive items), the customs will generally contact the recipient to provide invoices or customs clearance documents, and the customs clearance fees shall be borne by the recipient. The costs of return or destruction caused by failure to contact the recipient or the recipient's non-cooperation with customs clearance shall be borne by the recipient.
